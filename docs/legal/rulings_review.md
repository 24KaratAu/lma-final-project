Thank you, this is careful work. On most of these you have read the judgment more closely than my one-line notes did, and where we differ you are mostly right. My comments follow.

## Your three questions

**No. 4, Krishan Murgai.** Your reading is correct, and my note was wrong as a statement of the ratio. The majority decided the case on construction: the covenant applied to an employee who "leaves", which does not include one who is dismissed, so they did not need to decide s.27. A.P. Sen J alone held the covenant void under s.27. My note gave the proposition the case is usually cited for, which comes from his concurrence. Please record your version.

For the system, do not let No. 4 carry the proposition that a restraint after the term is void. Percept D'Mark (No. 6) is your authority for that, with Brahmaputra Tea (No. 2) as early support. No. 4 matters for leases on a different point: a restrictive covenant is read strictly, and only against the event it actually names. A clause that binds the tenant "on vacating" may not reach a tenant who has been evicted.

**No. 5, Gujarat Bottling.** Use the wording from the judgment: "unless the contract is unconscionable, excessively harsh, unreasonable or one-sided." The word "wholly" does not appear, and it raises the bar the court set. My note also left out "unreasonable". The formulation comes from Golikari, which Gujarat Bottling adopted, so Nos. 3 and 5 should use the same words.

**No. 10, Food Corporation of India.** Again your reading is correct. My note described the line of cases this decision sits in, not what it decided. The clause was about lodging the claim with the insurer. It did not shorten the time to sue, so a suit brought within the ordinary limitation period after a claim lodged in time was maintainable. Keep your sentence.

Two cautions for the system:
- The distinction between limiting the time to sue and extinguishing the right belongs to No. 11 (Sujir Ganesh Nayak), not No. 10.
- Both are pre-1997 cases. Under No. 12 (IndusInd Bank) they govern only agreements made before 8 January 1997. For a lease signed today, s.28(b) makes a clause that extinguishes rights or discharges liability after a set period void as well. Do not cite No. 10 as authority that a "claims must be notified within X days" clause in a current lease is valid under the amended s.28. These cases do not settle that, and the system should say so rather than guess.

## The rest

- **No. 1, Madhub Chunder.** I don't have a link I would trust. The report is in the Bengal Law Reports, so a law library is the reliable source. Percept D'Mark discusses the case at some length. Until you have read the original, record it as known through Percept D'Mark and not as read. Please also choose between your two bracketed alternatives before the email goes anywhere else.
- **No. 8, ABC Laminart.** Your summary of what was decided is accurate. On how such a clause is construed, though, Swastik Gases (No. 9) is now the governing authority. If both are shown next to a lease's jurisdiction clause, the system should rely on No. 9 for whether a "subject to the courts at X" clause excludes other courts.
- **No. 15, Vidya Drolia.** Correct. Please add that it overruled Himangni Enterprises (2017), which had held tenancy disputes under the Transfer of Property Act not arbitrable. Many lease drafts still rely on Himangni, so the point matters.
- **No. 18, CORE.** The neutral citation 2024 INSC 857 is acceptable. Add an SCC citation once the case is reported, but don't hold anything up for it. You have also left out something important: the Constitution Bench applied its ruling prospectively, to arbitrator appointments made after the date of the judgment. Your sentence should say so. Otherwise the system will flag existing appointments it should not.
- **Nos. 2, 3, 6, 7, 9, 11–14, 16 and 17.** I have no corrections. Each states the holding correctly and no more.

With these changes, please send me the revised sheet and I will sign it off.
